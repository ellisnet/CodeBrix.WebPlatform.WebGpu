================================================================================
THIRD-PARTY NOTICES: the crates that the CodeBrix.WebPlatform.WebGpu.Animation
package brings to an application's WebAssembly file
================================================================================

This file travels with the CodeBrix.WebPlatform.WebGpu.Animation.ApacheLicenseForever
package (tools/webgpu-animation-crates/THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES-WebGpuAnimationCrates.txt;
a build program finds it beside itself as
webgpu-animation-crates/THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES-WebGpuAnimationCrates.txt and reads
it with AnimationCrates.ReadNotices). It covers the package's crate bundle: the
archive webgpu-animation-crates.zip, which the platform's frontend build writes
into the application's frontend beside the platform's own crates and the WebGpu
package's.

The archive holds codebrix-webgpu-animation, the first-party crate of the
CodeBrix.WebPlatform.WebGpu repository (real-time 2D animation and its
renderer, and its text), and the third-party crates that ONLY that crate
needs: `kurbo` (curves, flattening and strokes), `peniko` (brushes, gradients,
images and blend modes), `harfrust` (text shaping and glyph outlines, with
`read-fonts` and `font-types`), `i_overlay` (path combining, with `i_float`,
`i_shape`, `i_tree` and `i_key_sort`), `usvg` (SVG documents, with its
`text` feature: `fontdb`, `skrifa` and the crates part F names), `velato`
(Lottie documents, with `serde_repr`) and the crates their resolution adds
beyond those the platform and the WebGpu package already carry. The crates it
builds on (wgpu, glam, codebrix-webgpu, and png with its tree, which reads the
PNG files of ImageSource::from_png) and the crates it shares with them
(arrayvec, smallvec, ...)
are carried by the CodeBrix.WebPlatform.WebGpu package and the platform, and
their notices come with those (WebGpuCrates.ReadNotices,
PlatformCrates.ReadNotices). Part 1 below lists the third-party crates of this
archive that are compiled into an application's WebAssembly file when its
frontend uses codebrix-webgpu-animation; Part 2 those that are carried but not
compiled in.

Each third-party component stays under its own license; none is relicensed.
Where a crate offers a choice of licenses ("Apache-2.0 OR MIT"), the entry
says which one is used.

codebrix-webgpu-animation is covered by the repository's own LICENSE
(Apache-2.0) and is not third-party, so it is not listed in the parts below.
Its renderer, however, contains code taken from the vello project, and inside
that code a few lines that vello marks as coming from Skia. They are compiled
into the application's WebAssembly file (the shaders as text, the rest as
code), stay under their own licenses, and are listed here.

A. Code taken from vello (the Linebender project) into codebrix-webgpu-animation
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Source:  the crates vello_shaders 0.12.0 and vello_encoding 0.12.0 as crates.io
         published them (https://crates.io/crates/vello_shaders,
         https://crates.io/crates/vello_encoding; project
         https://github.com/linebender/vello). Neither crate, nor vello itself,
         is a dependency of codebrix-webgpu-animation or carried by this
         package: the code below was taken into the crate's own files.
License: the crates declare "Apache-2.0 OR MIT"; the shader files carry the
         header "SPDX-License-Identifier: Apache-2.0 OR MIT OR Unlicense" and
         ramp_cache.rs "SPDX-License-Identifier: Apache-2.0 OR MIT".
         USED UNDER Apache-2.0. The Apache License 2.0 text is the LICENSE file
         of the CodeBrix.WebPlatform.WebGpu repository, and it is also among
         the licence texts at the end of this file (the LICENSE-APACHE of the
         crates below that are used under Apache-2.0).
Copyright 2022 the Vello Authors (the files' headers; the crates' LICENSE-MIT
says "Copyright 2020 the Vello Authors").

Taken, and where:
  - src/rendering/core/composite.wgsl: COPIED VERBATIM - everything from
    "// Color mixing modes" to the end of vello_shaders-0.12.0/shader/shared/
    blend.wgsl (the mix and compose mode constants and functions, screen,
    color_dodge, color_burn, hard_light, soft_light, the saturation and
    luminosity helpers, blend_mix, blend_compose, unpremultiply,
    blend_mix_compose). The file keeps vello's copyright and SPDX lines above
    the copied block.
  - src/rendering/core/paint.wgsl: TRANSLATED AND ADAPTED from
    vello_shaders-0.12.0/shader/fine.wgsl - extend_mode_normalized and the
    extend constants (copied), the radial-gradient parameter (the strip,
    focal-on-circle, cone and circular kinds) and the sweep-gradient angle,
    rewritten as functions of one pixel.
  - src/rendering/core/paint.rs: TRANSLATED TO RUST from
    vello_shaders-0.12.0/shader/draw_leaf.wgsl (the two-point conical
    gradient set-up, two_point_to_unit_line, from_poly2, the sweep set-up, the
    radial kind constants of shader/shared/config.wgsl), and ADAPTED from
    vello_encoding-0.12.0/src/ramp_cache.rs (make_ramp, the gradient ramp of
    512 samples, and the idea of a ramp cache whose slots are reused least
    recently used first).
  - src/rendering/core/common.wgsl is the crate's own; its header names the
    vello material of the two shader files above.

Credits that vello itself gives inside that material (no code of theirs beyond
what is listed in B):
  - The two-point conical gradient set-up is "based on the algorithm at
    https://skia.org/docs/dev/design/conical/" - an algorithm described in a
    design document of the Skia project.
  - The blend and compose formulas are those of the W3C specification
    "Compositing and Blending Level 1".

G. Lottie documents: velato and serde_repr
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

velato 0.12.0 (the Linebender project; "Apache-2.0 OR MIT", USED UNDER
Apache-2.0) is compiled into an application's WebAssembly file when it uses
this crate's Lottie module: it reads a Lottie document's JSON into its
composition and evaluates it for a frame. It is used WITH ITS DEFAULT FEATURES
OFF, so vello and its crates are not in the bundle. Its AUTHORS file lists its
significant contributors: Google LLC, Chad Brokaw, Daniel McNab, Spencer C.
Imbleau, Bruce Mitchener, Sebastian Hamel and Kaur Kuut. Its LICENSE-MIT holds
the MIT text without a copyright line; its LICENSE-APACHE is the Apache License
2.0 reproduced in Part 1. velato is used exactly as published: the crate's own
pass over the JSON runs before velato reads a document (see the Lottie section
of the repository's AGENT-README.txt), and nothing of velato is changed.

serde_repr 0.1.21 ("MIT OR Apache-2.0", USED UNDER Apache-2.0) is a procedural
macro that velato's reader uses: it runs while the application is BUILT and
generates code for velato's number-valued enumerations. It carries a GitHub
workflow file and a funding file of its own project (.github/workflows/ci.yml,
.github/FUNDING.yml) and compile-failure test expectations (tests/ui); they are
part of the crate as published, never compiled and never run.

velato also uses serde, serde_json and the crates they bring; those are the
platform's (and serde's derive the WebGpu package's), at the same versions, and
their notices come with them.
